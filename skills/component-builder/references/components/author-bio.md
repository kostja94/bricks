# Author Bio

## Responsibility

An author bio gives readers a concise, verifiable explanation of who created or reviewed content and why that person's background matters here. It complements the byline and author page rather than duplicating the complete profile.

## Content Contract

Use a stable author source shared with the visible byline and any structured article data. Typical fields include name, profile URL, image, short biography, relevant role or credential, organization, and a small number of verifiable profile links. Support multiple contributors and explicit roles such as written by, reviewed by, or fact-checked by when the editorial process actually uses them.

## Context Decisions

- Long-form articles commonly place the full bio after the body.
- High-risk or expertise-sensitive content may also show a compact credential near the title.
- A name-only byline is often enough when no meaningful bio exists.
- Organization authors require a real accountable organization identity, not a fictional person.

## Constraints

- Do not invent credentials, experience, images, profiles, or review roles.
- Do not create an empty card around a name when the byline already serves the same purpose.
- Structured data must agree with visible authorship. Entity identifiers can support consistency but are not universally required.
- AI assistance may be disclosed as part of the publishing process, but it must not replace the accountable author with a fictional human identity.

## Verify

Check consistency with the byline and author destination, missing image or biography behavior, multiple contributors, long names and translations, keyboard links, image dimensions, and structured data when the page emits it.
