# Footer

## Responsibility

A footer closes a page or product surface with secondary navigation, required organization or legal information, and a small number of context-appropriate continuation paths. It is not a dumping ground for every link in the product.

## Context Decisions

- Marketing sites may need product, resource, company, and legal groups.
- Content sites may emphasize taxonomy, newsletter, authorship, and archive discovery.
- Application footers are usually compact and may contain only status, legal, version, or support access.
- Internal tools may omit a global footer when persistent navigation already owns these functions.

## Variants

- Minimal: copyright, legal, and one support or contact path.
- Grouped: several labeled navigation groups with a clear hierarchy.
- Conversion-aware: grouped navigation plus one restrained newsletter or product action.
- Product utility: status, documentation, version, keyboard help, or environment information.

Choose one from the actual information architecture. These are variants of Footer, not separate component identities.

## Constraints

- Link labels must remain understandable outside visual grouping.
- Do not repeat the full primary navigation without a user need.
- Legal and organization identity must reflect the real operator.
- Newsletter or social controls must not dominate the page's completed primary task.
- On mobile, preserve group labels and reading order; collapsible groups require accessible disclosure behavior.

## Verify

Check long labels, multiple languages, keyboard traversal, external-link behavior, mobile grouping, current year or version ownership, and whether every link has a maintained destination.
